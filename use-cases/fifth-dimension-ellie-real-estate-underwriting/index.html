Fifth Dimension "Ellie": how the agent works and how to build one like it

Summary: Fifth Dimension's "Ellie" handles real estate deal screening and underwriting in 5–10 minutes, per the source.

Source: https://cloud.google.com/transform/101-real-world-generative-ai-use-cases-from-industry-leaders source page fetched

Why this use case works

Investors screen many more deals than they close, so fast first-pass underwriting means more deals evaluated with the same team and quicker answers to brokers. (Our analysis.)

Agent pattern: Document-review agent

A document-processing agent: ingestion and OCR, extraction into a schema, rule or model-based review, and a human approval step before anything binding goes out. Audit logs are a requirement, not a nice-to-have.

Who this fits

Who this is most relevant for: small multifamily and commercial investors, family offices and acquisition teams that receive more broker offering memorandums than they can read. The quick first pass matters most when the buy box is clearly defined, for example by market, unit count, price range and minimum yield, so that a deal can be screened against explicit criteria. (Our analysis.)

How a smaller team could replicate it

Small real estate investors and property managers can automate first-pass screening: extract rent rolls and operating statements, compute standard metrics, and flag deals that meet the buy box for full human underwriting. (Our analysis, not a claim made by the source.)

Want a rough business case first? Try the AI agent ROI calculator, or ask us to scope it.

Risks and guardrails

Risks: bad source data (rent rolls, T-12s) producing confident but wrong metrics. Guardrails: flag inconsistencies between documents, show the inputs behind every metric, and require full human underwriting before any offer. (Our analysis.)

What to measure

Deals screened per week, time per screen, deals advanced, and accuracy against full underwriting.
