MHK SmartProminence: how the agent works and how to build one like it

Summary: MHK's SmartProminence is a HIPAA-eligible agent orchestrator that cut manual medical review by 90%, per the source.

Source: https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/architecture/how-mhk-built-a-hipaa-eligible-agentic-ai-solution-on-amazon-bedrock/ source read via search extract

Why this use case works

Medical review mixes document reading with policy rules, which suits an orchestrator that routes cases: auto-approve the clear ones, send the unclear ones to clinicians. The 90% figure reflects how much of the volume is routine. (Our analysis.)

Agent pattern: Document-review agent

A document-processing agent: ingestion and OCR, extraction into a schema, rule or model-based review, and a human approval step before anything binding goes out. Audit logs are a requirement, not a nice-to-have.

How a smaller team could replicate it

Health plans and utilization-management vendors should start with a HIPAA-eligible stack, automate evidence gathering and criteria matching, and keep any denial a human decision. (Our analysis, not a claim made by the source.)

Risks and guardrails

Risks: wrongful denials of care and protected health information exposure. Guardrails: only humans issue denials, HIPAA-eligible infrastructure with business associate agreements, and audits of auto-approved cases. (Our analysis.)

What to measure

Share of reviews handled automatically, clinician time per case, overturn rate on appeal, and turnaround time.
