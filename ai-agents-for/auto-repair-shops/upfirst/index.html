Upfirst for auto repair shops: setup outline and ROI preset

Upfirst prices by answered call, starting at $24.95, which makes it a low-risk first AI receptionist for a one- or two-bay shop. Its $9.95 add-on agent also suits owners running a second location.

What Upfirst documents that matters here

  • Upfirst's pricing page lists Starter at $24.95 a month for 30 calls ($1.50 per additional call), Premium at $59.95 for 90 ($1), Pro at $159.95 for 300 ($0.75) and Scale at $299 for 600 ($0.70); annual billing saves 20%. Source official page fetched, 2026-10-28
  • Plans list warm call transfers, appointment scheduling into Google, Outlook, Acuity, Calendly, Square and other calendars, in-call texting, spam blocking, memory of returning callers and a Zapier integration. Source official page fetched, 2026-10-28
  • Each additional agent, for another business or location, is $9.95 a month, and training can be shared or kept separate. Source official page fetched, 2026-10-28

Why it fits auto repair shops

A service writer at the counter cannot answer the phone while walking a customer through a brake quote. Most shop calls are short and repetitive: is my car ready, do you do alignments, can I bring it in Thursday. Per-call billing fits that. In-call texting lets the agent send a booking link or directions mid-call, and memory of returning callers helps with the repeat 'is it ready' calls that eat a writer's morning. (Our analysis.)

What it costs at auto repair shops volumes

Our arithmetic from the posted rates: a one-bay shop taking 250 calls a month fits Pro at $159.95. A multi-bay shop at 500 calls would pay $159.95 plus 200 extra calls at $0.75 ($309.95) on Pro, so Scale at $299 is slightly cheaper.

Setup outline for auto repair shops

  1. Count a month of calls and sort them: status checks, new appointments, quote requests, parts and warranty calls.
  2. Write the intake fields: year, make, model, mileage, symptom, and whether the car is drivable or needs a tow.
  3. Decide what the agent may say about prices: perhaps fixed-price services like oil changes and inspections, never diagnostic estimates.
  4. Connect the calendar you book from, or send call summaries by Zapier to your shop software.
  5. Use the free trial on overflow and after-hours calls, with warm transfer for tow-ins and unhappy customers.
  6. Add a second location as an extra agent with its own hours and address only after the first one works.

Watch-outs

  • Status calls need job-status data; without shop-software integration the agent can only take a message.
  • Ask whether voicemails and early hang-ups count as answered calls.
  • Phone price quotes create disputes; keep the agent to fixed-price items.
  • Recheck the tier after a full month of real volume.
