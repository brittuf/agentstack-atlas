Dialzara for auto repair shops: setup outline and ROI preset

A repair shop's phone rings hardest when the service writers are under a car or at the counter. Dialzara is a per-minute receptionist with tiers that matter for shops: the plan you pick decides whether you get calendar sync, after-hours rules and warm transfers, not just message-taking.

What Dialzara documents that matters here

  • Dialzara's pricing page lists Business Lite at $29/month (60 minutes), Pro at $99 (220), Plus at $199 (500) and Elite at $349 (1,000), with overage of $0.48, $0.45, $0.40 and $0.35 per minute. Source official page fetched, 2026-11-11
  • Calendar sync (Google or Outlook) and after-hours rules with escalation start on Pro; Lite does not include them, and its knowledge base allows 5 uploads against 10 on Pro and unlimited on Plus. Source official page fetched, 2026-11-11
  • Every plan lists call recordings and summaries, 10 languages including English and Spanish, a US phone number and Zapier/Make connections; there is a 7-day free trial and no contract. Source official page fetched, 2026-11-11

Why it fits auto repair shops

Auto repair calls are mostly three things: 'is my car ready', 'can you fit me in this week' and 'how much for brakes'. The first and third are information; the second needs a calendar. Because Dialzara bills by the minute and these calls are short, a shop with a modest call count can sit on a mid-tier plan. Spanish support on every plan helps in many shop neighborhoods. The paid tier that adds calendar sync is the one to test, since appointment requests are where a shop actually earns from the call. (Our analysis.)

What it costs at auto repair shops volumes

Our arithmetic from the posted rates: 250 calls a month at 3 minutes is 750 minutes. Plus at $199 plus 250 extra minutes at $0.40 comes to $299; Pro would be $99 plus 530 extra minutes at $0.45, about $338; Elite is $349. If you only cover after-hours calls, say 100 a month at 3 minutes (300 minutes), Pro at $99 plus 80 extra minutes at $0.45 is about $135.

Setup outline for auto repair shops

  1. Count one month of calls and note how many are status checks, quote requests and booking requests; multiply total calls by your average minutes.
  2. Start the 7-day trial on Pro so calendar sync and after-hours rules are available during testing.
  3. Load the knowledge base with hours, diagnostic fee, shuttle or loaner policy, brands you service and what you do not work on.
  4. Connect the shop calendar and decide how many bays-worth of appointments the agent may offer per day.
  5. Route status questions to a message with the vehicle and plate, and send warm transfers only for tow-ins, angry callers and fleet accounts.
  6. After the trial week, multiply minutes used by four and choose between Pro, Plus and Elite.

Watch-outs

  • Status calls ('is my car done') still need a human answer unless your shop software feeds the status to the agent; plan a message-taking fallback.
  • Per-minute billing rises when callers ramble about symptoms; read a few transcripts for length.
  • Never let the agent quote repair prices unless you wrote the number into the knowledge base.
  • Check that the calendar it offers matches real bay capacity, not just open hours.
