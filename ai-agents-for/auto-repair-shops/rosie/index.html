Rosie for auto repair shops: setup outline and ROI preset

Rosie is a small-business answering service with generous minutes at low prices. For a repair shop, the question is which of three tiers adds the pieces that matter: texting an appointment link, booking directly on your calendar, or chasing several numbers until a person answers.

What Rosie documents that matters here

  • Rosie's pricing page lists Professional at $49/month for 250 minutes, Scale at $149 for 1,000 minutes and Growth at $299 for 2,000 minutes, with a 7-day free trial. Source official page fetched, 2026-11-11
  • Professional can text appointment links; Scale adds booking directly on your calendar, warm transfers and sending texts to callers during the call; Growth adds waterfall transfers across several numbers and custom agent training from uploaded documents. Source official page fetched, 2026-11-11
  • All plans include English and Spanish on every call, the ability to handle multiple calls simultaneously, call summaries, transcripts and recordings, native iOS and Android apps and a Zapier integration. Source official page fetched, 2026-11-11

Why it fits auto repair shops

A shop that is closed on Sundays still gets Sunday calls from people with a dead battery or a warning light. Rosie's 250-minute entry plan works as an overnight message taker; the step up to Scale is justified only if you want the agent to put appointments on the calendar itself. Handling many calls at once means the Monday-morning pile-up does not turn into busy signals. Waterfall transfers are a Growth feature, so a small shop with one owner-operator who wants calls to chase his phone and his partner's should price that tier knowingly. (Our analysis.)

What it costs at auto repair shops volumes

Our arithmetic from the posted minutes: 250 calls at 3 minutes is 750 minutes a month, which exceeds Professional's 250 and fits inside Scale's 1,000 at $149. A shop that only forwards after-hours calls, say 80 calls at 3 minutes (240 minutes), fits Professional at $49. Rosie's page does not list a per-minute overage rate in the text we checked, so confirm what happens past your included minutes.

Setup outline for auto repair shops

  1. Total a month of calls and minutes before choosing a tier; 750 minutes rules out the $49 plan.
  2. Start the free trial and run it as after-hours and overflow only (Rosie's FAQ has a question on that; confirm the answer with them).
  3. Teach it your hours, services, diagnostic fee and the cars you will not touch, then test with five scripted calls.
  4. Decide whether texting a booking link (Professional) is enough, or whether you need direct calendar booking (Scale).
  5. Define transfer rules: warm transfer to the service manager for fleet accounts and angry customers.
  6. Review summaries for a week and adjust the custom message-taking scenarios, which are limited to 2 on Professional and 5 on Scale.

Our suggested order, not vendor documentation.

Watch-outs

  • The page we checked did not state an overage rate, so ask before you exceed your minutes.
  • Custom message-taking scenarios are capped by plan; map your top intake questions to the limit.
  • A texted booking link depends on the customer finishing the booking, unlike direct calendar booking.
  • Test how it handles a caller describing a noise; it should take notes, not diagnose.
