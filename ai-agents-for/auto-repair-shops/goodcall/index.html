Goodcall for auto repair shops: setup outline and ROI preset

Goodcall bills by unique customer per month, not by minute or call. For an auto shop, where one customer may ring three times about a single repair, that changes the maths.

What Goodcall documents that matters here

  • Goodcall's pricing page lists Starter at $79 a month for 100 unique customers (79 cents each after), Growth at $129 for 500 (26 cents) and Scale at $299 for 2,000 (15 cents). Source official page fetched, 2026-10-28
  • It defines a unique customer as one phone number once a month, so five calls from the same person in March count once; spam and wrong numbers do not count, and minutes are never metered. Source official page fetched, 2026-10-28
  • Every plan includes a local number, or you can forward your existing one; Starter allows one external tool connection and Growth five. Source official page fetched, 2026-10-28

Why it fits auto repair shops

Repair calls cluster around the same people: the drop-off call, the 'what did you find' call, the approval call and the 'is it ready' call. Per-minute and per-call plans bill each one. Goodcall bills the person once a month, so a shop whose phone is busy with existing jobs rather than new leads may pay less here. Long calls, such as explaining a check-engine light, cost nothing extra. (Our analysis.)

What it costs at auto repair shops volumes

Our arithmetic: if a one-bay shop's 250 monthly calls come from about 150 different numbers (an assumption; check your log), Starter costs $79 plus 50 extra customers at 79 cents, or $118.50, while Growth at $129 covers up to 500. A multi-bay shop with 500 calls from about 300 numbers also fits Growth at $129.

Setup outline for auto repair shops

  1. Export a month of call logs and count unique phone numbers, not calls; that number picks your plan.
  2. Build the free demo agent with your hours, services, towing policy and intake questions for a new repair.
  3. Decide how status calls work: a callback request to the service writer, or a lookup if you connect shop software as your external tool.
  4. Forward overflow calls first and keep your existing number, routing questions about cars already in the bay to staff during business hours.
  5. Listen to a week of calls and list every question the agent could not answer.
  6. After a month, compare the unique customers billed with your own log to confirm the count.

Our suggested order, not vendor documentation.

Watch-outs

  • If most callers are new each time and calls are short, per-call plans may be cheaper; compare both.
  • External tool limits per plan matter once you connect shop software and a calendar.
  • Ask how a customer who calls from two numbers is counted.
  • Keep a person on calls about vehicles already being worked on.
