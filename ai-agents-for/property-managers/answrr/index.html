Answrr for property managers: setup outline and ROI preset

Property managers juggle leasing inquiries by day and maintenance emergencies by night, often across many buildings. Answrr's plan limits on receptionists (3, 10 or 25) map neatly onto a portfolio, and its agency plans let a management company or its vendor run up to 100 receptionists from one minute pool.

What Answrr documents that matters here

  • Answrr's pricing page lists up to 3 receptionists on Solo ($49), 10 on Growth ($99) and 25 on Pro ($199, 1,600 minutes, $0.15/min top-ups). Source official page fetched, 2026-11-18
  • Agency Reseller is listed at $247/month with 2,000 pooled minutes split between clients, top-ups at $0.10/min and up to 100 receptionists with client portals on your own domain. Source official page fetched, 2026-11-18
  • The features page says agents can be split by phone number, location, service or after-hours versus business hours, each with a separate knowledge base, calendar and transfer rules. Source official page fetched, 2026-11-18

Why it fits property managers

A building-specific agent can answer the questions residents and prospects actually ask: pet policy, parking, laundry, application fees, showing times. The after-hours agent has a different job: separate a true emergency (no heat, water leak, lockout) from a request that can wait, and transfer the emergencies to the on-call maintenance number. Keeping each property's rules in its own knowledge base avoids quoting the wrong deposit at the wrong address. (Our analysis.)

What it costs at property managers volumes

Our arithmetic from the posted rates: 500 calls a month at 4 minutes is 2,000 minutes. Pro would be $199 plus 400 top-up minutes at $0.15, $259, and allows up to 25 receptionists. A portfolio needing more than 25 agents, or a vendor serving several management companies, would compare the $247 Agency Reseller plan with its 2,000 pooled minutes.

Setup outline for property managers

  1. List your properties and decide whether each gets its own number and agent or shares one line.
  2. Write one knowledge base per property: fees, pet policy, parking, office hours and showing rules.
  3. Build a separate after-hours agent whose only job is emergency triage and transfer to on-call maintenance.
  4. Define emergency triggers with your maintenance lead: active leak, no heat in winter, gas smell, lockout, fire or flood.
  5. Send structured summaries for maintenance requests to your ticket system or a shared inbox by webhook.
  6. Review a week of after-hours transcripts with the maintenance lead before switching all buildings over.

Our suggested order, not vendor documentation.

Watch-outs

  • Fair-housing rules apply to what the agent says to prospects; have someone review the leasing script.
  • Do not let the agent promise repair times; it should log and route.
  • A per-property setup multiplies the knowledge bases you must keep current.
  • We found no named property-management software integration; plan on webhooks or MCP/API work.
