Upfirst for property managers: setup outline and ROI preset

Upfirst lists property management as a named industry, and its plan mix suits a small manager who gets a few hundred tenant and prospect calls a month. The decision points are how urgent maintenance calls escalate and how many languages your residents need.

What Upfirst documents that matters here

  • Upfirst's pricing page lists Pro at $159.95/month for 300 calls (75¢ per extra call), Scale at $299 for 600 (70¢) and Premium at $59.95 for 90 calls; annual billing is about 20% lower. Source official page fetched, 2026-11-11
  • Its navigation lists Property management among industries and states it handles tenant calls and urgent issues around the clock; features on every plan include waterfall transfers, two-way texting and 35+ languages. Source official page fetched, 2026-11-11
  • The page says spam never counts toward call volume, the trial is 14 days with no card, and calendar scheduling works with Google, Outlook, Clio, Acuity, Calendly and Square. Source official page fetched, 2026-11-11

Why it fits property managers

Tenant calls split into emergencies and everything else: a burst pipe at midnight versus a question about the lease or the parking spot. Waterfall transfers let you set an order such as maintenance supervisor, then backup, then owner. Texting back a showing link or a maintenance-request form saves a callback. Language support matters for portfolios with a mixed resident base, since the transcript can be translated for you. (Our analysis.)

What it costs at property managers volumes

Our arithmetic from the posted prices: 300 calls a month on Pro is $159.95 billed monthly, or $128 on the annual rate. A 450-call month would add 150 calls at 75¢ (about $113) to Pro, about $272, which is close to Scale's $299 for 600.

Setup outline for property managers

  1. Sort a month of tenant and prospect calls into emergencies, maintenance requests, leasing questions and billing.
  2. Start the 14-day trial and define what counts as an emergency: no heat, no water, flooding, fire alarms, lockouts.
  3. Set the waterfall for emergencies and a message path for routine requests.
  4. Give it the leasing basics you want quoted: application process, pet policy, office hours; leave rent quotes to staff unless listed.
  5. Decide whether to text a maintenance-request link, and who owns the follow-up.
  6. After the trial, compare call volume against the 300-call Pro tier.

Watch-outs

  • Emergency definitions must be written down, not left to the agent.
  • Fair-housing rules apply to leasing conversations; keep screening decisions with staff.
  • Waterfall transfers need current phone numbers for the on-call crew.
  • Do not let it quote rent or fees unless those numbers are in the knowledge base.
