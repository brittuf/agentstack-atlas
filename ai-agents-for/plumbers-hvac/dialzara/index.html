Dialzara for plumbers & HVAC: setup outline and ROI preset

For a plumbing or HVAC shop that is not on ServiceTitan, Dialzara is one of the cheaper ways to stop sending night and weekend calls to voicemail. The plan choice hinges on one feature more than on price: whether an emergency caller gets a warm transfer to the on-call tech.

What Dialzara documents that matters here

  • Dialzara's pricing page lists Business Lite at $29/month for 60 minutes ($0.48 per extra minute), Pro at $99 for 220 minutes ($0.45), Plus at $199 for 500 ($0.40) and Elite at $349 for 1,000 ($0.35). Source official page fetched, 2026-10-28
  • Lite has blind transfer only; warm transfer starts on Pro. Zapier and Make integrations are on every plan, with a 7-day free trial and no contract. Source official page fetched, 2026-10-28
  • Elite adds multi-agent and multi-location setup, API access and initial prompt setup. Source official page fetched, 2026-10-28

Why it fits plumbers & HVAC

Home-service calls run longer than most: a caller describing a leak, giving an address, checking you cover their area. So minutes, not call count, set the bill. Warm transfer matters because the on-call tech hears 'basement flooding on Oak Street, customer is home' before picking up, instead of a cold ring at 2 a.m. Blind transfer on the entry plan just forwards the call with no context, and the caller repeats everything to a tired technician. What it costs at plumbers & HVAC volumes

Our arithmetic using the posted rates: a quiet month of 120 after-hours calls at 4 minutes is 480 minutes, which fits Plus at $199 (Pro would be $99 plus 260 extra minutes at $0.45, or $216). A busy season of 300 calls at 4 minutes is 1,200 minutes: Elite at $349 plus 200 minutes at $0.35 is $419, cheaper than Plus with 700 extra minutes ($479).

Setup outline for plumbers & HVAC

  1. Pull last winter's and last summer's call logs and total the talk minutes; HVAC volume swings by season and you may change plans twice a year.
  2. Start the 7-day trial on Pro rather than Lite, so you can test warm transfer on a real emergency scenario.
  3. Write the intake script: name, address, callback number, problem, whether water or gas is involved, and whether the caller is a member or past customer.
  4. Define the emergency words that trigger a warm transfer to the on-call phone, and what the agent says if nobody answers.
  5. Connect Zapier or Make so every call summary creates a lead or job request in Housecall Pro, Jobber or a shared sheet.
  6. Check minutes used at the end of the trial week and multiply by four before choosing Plus or Elite.

Watch-outs

  • Overage adds up in a heat wave; check usage weekly in peak season.
  • Dialzara takes messages and transfers; booking straight into dispatch software needs an integration step.
  • Self-editable prompts mean someone has to own updates when prices or service areas change.
  • Listen to what callers hear while a warm transfer rings through.
