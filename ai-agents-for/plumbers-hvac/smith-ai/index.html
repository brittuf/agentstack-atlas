Smith.ai for plumbers & HVAC: setup outline and ROI preset

Smith.ai charges per call, which fits home-service work: a long emergency call costs the same as a quick one. It also lists a live North America-based receptionist as backup, which is unusual and relevant when a caller has a flooded kitchen.

What Smith.ai documents that matters here

  • Smith.ai's AI receptionist page lists Free at 25 calls a month ($3.00 per extra call), and Pro at $150 for 75 calls, $270 for 150 and $500 for 300, with extra calls at $2.50, $2.30 and $2.17. Source official page fetched, 2026-11-11
  • Its FAQ says calls are handled by AI first but can be transferred to a live North America-based receptionist at any time, day or night; plans are month-to-month with 30 days' notice to cancel. Source official page fetched, 2026-11-11
  • It says over 20 million known spam numbers are filtered before the AI answers, and AI Receptionist customers can remove up to 10% of calls from their bill each cycle for selected spam. Source official page fetched, 2026-11-11
  • All plans list AI scheduling (Calendly and more), call recording and transcription, lead qualification and call routing, and a Quality Studio for test calls before going live. Source official page fetched, 2026-11-11

Why it fits plumbers & HVAC

Plumbing and HVAC callers are often stressed and the call is worth a lot if you answer it. Per-call billing avoids paying for a caller who needs two minutes to describe a leak. The human backup is the feature to price: for an after-hours emergency a caller can be sent to a person instead of an agent trying to improvise. The spam allowance matters because home-service numbers attract robocalls that would otherwise use up your quota. (Our analysis.)

What it costs at plumbers & HVAC volumes

Our arithmetic from the posted rates: 150 after-hours and overflow calls a month fits the $270 Pro tier. At 200 calls you would pay $270 plus 50 extra calls at $2.30, about $385, versus $500 for the 300-call tier. The free plan lets you test with 25 real calls first.

Setup outline for plumbers & HVAC

  1. Run the free plan for two weeks with Quality Studio test calls for no heat, burst pipe, water heater quote and member tune-up.
  2. Write the emergency rules: gas smell, flooding or no heat below a temperature you choose goes to the live receptionist or the on-call tech.
  3. Confirm in writing how the transfer to a human receptionist works and whether it carries an extra cost.
  4. Connect scheduling or Zapier so each call creates a job request in your dispatch tool or a shared sheet.
  5. Mark obvious spam calls each week to use the 10% bill allowance and keep the spam list current.
  6. Count real calls after two weeks, then choose between 75, 150 or 300 calls a month.

Watch-outs

  • The human-backup arrangement is described in the FAQ; check whether human transfers change your bill.
  • Per-call billing means a day of repeat callers can eat your quota.
  • Booking straight into dispatch software needs an integration step; do not assume it exists.
  • A cheap-looking free tier ends at 25 calls, so a busy week can cost $3 per call.
