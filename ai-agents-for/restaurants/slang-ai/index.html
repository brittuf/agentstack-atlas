Slang AI for restaurants: setup outline and ROI preset

Slang AI is built for the phone at a full-service restaurant: reservations, changes and the same guest questions every night. This page sets out a setup order for a dining room, the Core-versus-Premium decision, and a calculator preset for a reservation-heavy restaurant.

What Slang AI documents that matters here

  • Slang AI's product page says guests can make, change or cancel reservations across OpenTable, Yelp and SevenRooms, and that the agent offers nearby or sister locations when the first choice is unavailable. Source official page fetched, 2026-10-28
  • Its pricing page lists Core from $399 per location and Premium from $599 per location; Premium adds 97-language Superhost conversations, private-events capture, real-time alerts and reservation cross-selling. Source official page fetched, 2026-10-28
  • The product page says the agent answers from the restaurant's approved information, and that call summaries, recordings and transcripts sit in one place for staff. Source official page fetched, 2026-10-28

Why it fits restaurants

A host stand cannot answer the phone and greet a walk-in at the same moment, and the calls that get dropped are often the valuable ones: a party of eight, a birthday, a private event. Slang's design suits that problem: book what can be booked straight into the reservation book you already use, and flag what needs a manager. Private-event capture sits in Premium, so that is the tier to price if events are a real share of revenue. (Our analysis.)

What it costs at restaurants volumes

Slang quotes by location. At the listed starting prices, a two-location group is looking at about $798 a month on Core or $1,198 on Premium before any negotiated terms (our arithmetic from the 'starting at' figures).

Setup outline for restaurants

  1. Tally a week of calls by reason: new reservations, changes, hours and directions, takeout, events. If takeout dominates, look at an ordering agent first.
  2. Confirm your reservation platform and table rules (party-size limits, deposits, holiday menus) are reachable through the integration.
  3. Write the approved answers: dress code, parking, corkage, allergy policy, children, accessibility. The agent answers from these, so gaps turn into transfers.
  4. Decide which calls always go to a person: large parties, complaints, press, private dining.
  5. Go live for the hours the host stand is busiest, usually the dinner rush, before it takes every call.
  6. After four weeks, compare covers booked by phone, abandoned calls and event inquiries with the prior four weeks.

Our suggested order, not vendor documentation.

Watch-outs

  • Plan how takeout calls are handled: a transfer or a texted ordering link.
  • Update holiday hours and special menus a week ahead.
  • Per-location pricing grows with every site you add.
  • Check that bookings respect deposits and cancellation policies.
