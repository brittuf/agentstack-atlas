Upfirst for restaurants: setup outline and ROI preset

Upfirst names restaurants as an industry and lists order taking among its solutions. For a small restaurant the useful question is how its per-call plans compare with the calls you get, and what the page does and does not say about ordering.

What Upfirst documents that matters here

  • Upfirst's pricing page lists Pro at $159.95/month for 300 calls (75¢ per extra call) and Scale at $299 for 600 calls (70¢), with annual billing about 20% lower and a 14-day free trial. Source official page fetched, 2026-11-11
  • Its navigation lists Restaurants among industries and Order taking among included solutions; the pricing page does not name POS or reservation-system integrations. Source official page fetched, 2026-11-11
  • Features on every plan include simultaneous calls so nobody hears a busy signal, 35+ languages, two-way texting and spam blocking that does not count toward volume. Source official page fetched, 2026-11-11

Why it fits restaurants

A restaurant's busiest calls arrive in the same hour its staff is busiest. Answering many callers at once removes the busy signal, and texting a menu or reservation link can handle the rest without a call. The page's mention of order taking is a feature name, so test it with real menu items rather than assume it matches your POS. Multiple languages help in dining rooms with mixed staff and guests. (Our analysis.)

What it costs at restaurants volumes

Our arithmetic from the posted prices: 500 calls a month is Pro at $159.95 plus 200 extra calls at 75¢, about $310, while Scale at $299 for 600 calls is cheaper. At 300 calls Pro alone covers it.

Setup outline for restaurants

  1. Tally a week of calls by reason: reservations, takeout, hours, events.
  2. Start the 14-day trial and ask Upfirst how order taking reaches your kitchen, since the page does not say.
  3. Load the menu, dietary notes, hours and large-party rules into the knowledge base.
  4. Test with 10 scripted orders including a modification and a sold-out item.
  5. Decide whether orders go to a text, an email or a ticket, and who sees it during service.
  6. Compare call count and extra-call charges after two weeks.

Watch-outs

  • The pricing page does not explain how orders reach a POS; get that answer in writing.
  • Per-call billing rises with long ordering calls; check an order's length in transcripts.
  • Update menu and sold-out items quickly.
  • Keep a person on the line path for complaints and events.
