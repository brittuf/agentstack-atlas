Dialzara for restaurants: setup outline and ROI preset

Dialzara is a general receptionist, not a restaurant system, so it fits one specific restaurant job: answering the phone when the host stand is busy or closed and capturing a message with the details you ask for. It does not document taking orders into a point-of-sale system.

What Dialzara documents that matters here

  • Dialzara lists Plus at $199/month for 500 minutes and Elite at $349 for 1,000 minutes, with overage of $0.40 and $0.35 per minute; all plans include message-taking with custom questions. Source official page fetched, 2026-11-11
  • Calendar sync, after-hours rules and warm transfers start on Pro; every plan lists 10 languages including English and Spanish and Zapier/Make integrations. Source official page fetched, 2026-11-11
  • Dialzara's page lists no restaurant reservation-system or POS integration; connections are via Zapier, Make or API, with a 7-day free trial. Source official page fetched, 2026-11-11

Why it fits restaurants

Restaurants get short calls: hours, parking, 'do you take reservations', catering and large-party inquiries. Message-taking with custom questions suits the last two, since a large party needs a date, headcount and callback number a manager can act on. Because the calls are short, a high call count still lands in a modest number of minutes. The limit is clear: this is an answering agent, so it will not seat a party in your reservation book unless you build a Zapier step. (Our analysis.)

What it costs at restaurants volumes

Our arithmetic from the posted rates: 500 calls at 2 minutes is 1,000 minutes, which exactly fills Elite at $349; Plus would be $199 plus 500 extra minutes at $0.40, $399. At 250 calls (500 minutes) Plus alone covers it at $199.

Setup outline for restaurants

  1. Count calls by reason for a week, and note which are reservation or catering inquiries versus simple questions.
  2. Start the trial on Pro so after-hours rules and warm transfer to a manager are available.
  3. Write the message-taking questions for catering: date, headcount, budget, callback number.
  4. Load hours, address, parking, dietary information and the private-dining policy.
  5. Use Zapier to send each catering or large-party message to the manager's email or a sheet.
  6. Review a week of transcripts and cut minutes by trimming how long the agent talks.

Our suggested order, not vendor documentation.

Watch-outs

  • No documented reservation-system integration; bookings need a Zapier step you build yourself.
  • Takeout orders are not covered by anything on the pricing page.
  • Allergy questions should go to staff unless your written answers are explicit.
  • Keep holiday hours updated a week ahead.
