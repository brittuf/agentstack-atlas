Tidio Lyro for restaurants: setup outline and ROI preset

Most restaurant questions arrive as Instagram and Facebook DMs or website chat, not phone calls: are you open on the holiday, do you take reservations for eight, is the fryer shared with gluten-free items. Tidio's Lyro answers in those same inboxes from the restaurant's own pages, and its quota counts conversations, which keeps a busy weekend from turning into a surprise bill.

What Tidio Lyro documents that matters here

  • Tidio's FAQ says Lyro replies in website live chat, email, Messenger, Instagram and WhatsApp, and when it hands over, staff pick up the same conversation. Source official page fetched, 2026-11-25
  • Per the pricing data, AI-enabled Growth costs $140 a month for a 200-conversation quota and $210 for 300, shared between Lyro and staff replies. Source official page fetched, 2026-11-25
  • The pricing FAQ says Lyro can learn from your website, your FAQ and articles you import, and that a Help Center add-on costs $10 a month if you want one place to keep those answers. Source official page fetched, 2026-11-25

Why it fits restaurants

A host stand cannot answer DMs during a Friday rush, and an unanswered message about a birthday dinner for twelve is a party that books somewhere else. Restaurant questions are repetitive and factual: hours, holiday closures, parking, corkage, private room minimums, gift cards, whether the patio is heated. Those answers live on the website already, which is exactly what Lyro reads. Allergy and dietary questions are the exception that needs care; the agent should quote the written policy and pass anything specific to a manager rather than improvise. Because Tidio bills per conversation rather than per resolution, a long back-and-forth about a large booking still costs one unit. (Our analysis.)

What it costs at restaurants volumes

Our arithmetic from Tidio's posted plan data: 180 DMs and chats a month fits the 200-conversation Growth quota at $140 on monthly billing, or about $117 a month billed annually with two months free. A quieter spot with under 100 conversations would pay $75. The Voice add-on (from $16 for 20 calls) can cover the phone line too, but each call counts against its own separate quota.

Setup outline for restaurants

  1. Pull a month of DMs and website chats and list the twenty questions guests ask most.
  2. Make sure the website states hours, holiday schedule, reservation policy, private dining minimums and allergen policy in plain sentences.
  3. Connect Instagram and Facebook Messenger first, since that is where most guest messages land, then the website widget.
  4. Write Guidance telling Lyro to hand any allergy detail, complaint or party over twelve to a manager.
  5. Use the 50 free Lyro conversations over a full weekend before you pick a quota.
  6. Review handoffs every Monday and add a sentence to the website for anything Lyro could not answer.

Watch-outs

  • Allergen answers must come from your written policy; tell Lyro to escalate anything specific to a dish.
  • Staff replies use the same quota as Lyro, so a team that chats a lot can run out mid-month.
  • Nothing on Tidio's pricing page says Lyro books tables; link your booking page, or connect a Smart Action if your reservation system supports one.
  • Holiday hours change: update the website first, because that is what Lyro reads.
