Answrr for restaurants: setup outline and ROI preset

Restaurant calls are short and repetitive: are you open, do you take reservations, is there parking, can I book for eight on Friday. Answrr's minute-based pricing rewards short calls, and its idle detection and call-length cap stop a dropped or silent line from running up the bill during a rush.

What Answrr documents that matters here

  • Answrr's features page lists idle detection with a 5 to 60 second timeout and up to three retry messages, and a maximum call duration setting of 1 to 60 minutes with a configurable goodbye. Source official page fetched, 2026-11-18
  • The same page says each agent can have its own phone number, knowledge base, calendar and transfer rules, for example separate locations or after-hours versus business hours. Source official page fetched, 2026-11-18
  • Answrr lists Growth at $99/month for 700 minutes with top-ups at $0.20/min, up to 10 receptionists. Source official page fetched, 2026-11-18

Why it fits restaurants

During service nobody wants to step away from the pass to answer the same question about hours. Calls that go unanswered at 6:30 p.m. are often reservations or large-party inquiries, the bookings worth having. Answrr's per-agent setup suits a group with two or three locations, each with its own number, menu documents and booking calendar. It is a reservation and question line, not an ordering system: we found no POS or online-ordering integration on its pages, so restaurants that live on phone orders should compare dedicated tools. What it costs at restaurants volumes

Our arithmetic from the posted rates: 600 calls a month averaging 1.5 minutes is 900 minutes. Growth would be $99 plus 200 top-up minutes at $0.20, $139. Solo would cost far more in top-ups ($211.50) and Pro's flat $199 only wins above about 1,200 minutes.

Setup outline for restaurants

  1. Count calls by hour for a typical week; most restaurants find the problem is a two-hour dinner window.
  2. Upload the menu, hours, holiday hours, parking and private-dining policy as the knowledge base.
  3. Decide whether reservations book directly into a calendar or the agent takes details for a host to confirm.
  4. Set a short maximum call length and idle timeout so silent or pocket-dial calls end quickly.
  5. Give each location its own agent and number if you run more than one.
  6. Check the summaries after each weekend and update the menu documents when specials change.

Watch-outs

  • No POS or phone-ordering integration is listed, so do not plan on it taking takeout orders into your system.
  • Reservation platforms differ; confirm how bookings reach yours before turning off the host's phone.
  • Menu and price changes must be re-uploaded, or the agent will quote old prices.
  • Large-party and event inquiries should transfer or create a callback for a manager.
