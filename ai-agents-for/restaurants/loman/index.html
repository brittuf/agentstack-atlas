Loman AI for restaurants: setup outline and ROI preset

Loman goes after the other half of restaurant calls: takeout and delivery orders. For a pizza shop or a takeout-heavy kitchen, the test is whether an agent can take a full order with modifiers and payment and land it in the POS as a clean ticket.

What Loman AI documents that matters here

  • Loman's October 2026 blog post says its agent takes complete orders with payment and syncs them into Toast, Square, Clover, Aloha, Olo, SpotOn, SkyTab (Shift4) and Stream, answers unlimited simultaneous calls and can go live in under 24 hours (vendor claims about its own product). Source official page fetched, 2026-10-28
  • Loman's pricing page lists takeout and delivery ordering, phone reservations, complete POS integration and secure payment over the phone in its Premium plan, and asks restaurants to request pricing. Source official page fetched, 2026-10-28

Why it fits restaurants

During a Friday rush a ringing phone costs twice: the order you might miss and the guest at the counter who waits while someone writes down a large pepperoni with half mushrooms. An ordering agent removes the interruption only if the ticket reaches the POS exactly as a staff member would have entered it, with sizes, modifiers and payment. Answering several calls at once also ends the busy signal in the hour when most orders arrive. (Our analysis.)

What it costs at restaurants volumes

Loman's blog says pricing starts at a flat $199 a month with no per-minute fees, while its pricing page asks for a quote and puts ordering and payments in Premium. Get the Premium price in writing before you run the numbers; the preset uses $199 only as a starting point.

Setup outline for restaurants

  1. Confirm your POS is on Loman's supported list and ask whether orders arrive as normal tickets with modifiers intact.
  2. Clean the menu in the POS first: consistent item names, sizes, modifiers and combo rules, because the agent works from that data.
  3. Choose your phone payment rule (pay on the call, at pickup, or either) and check how card data is handled.
  4. Place 20 test orders covering half-and-half pizzas, substitutions, an allergy question and a sold-out item.
  5. Switch the agent on for overflow during one busy dinner service, with a staff member watching tickets print.
  6. Track phone revenue, average ticket, remakes and complaints for four weeks against the previous four.

Our suggested order, not vendor documentation.

Watch-outs

  • The $199 figure comes from Loman's blog, not its pricing page.
  • Send allergy questions to staff unless the menu data answers them clearly.
  • Sold-out items must update quickly or the agent sells food you do not have.
  • Upsell prompts can annoy regulars; listen to calls and tune them.
