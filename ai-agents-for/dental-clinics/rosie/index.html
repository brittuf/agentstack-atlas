Rosie for dental & medical clinics: setup outline and ROI preset

Rosie is a low-cost AI answering service with English and Spanish on every plan. For a dental office the plan decision comes down to one question: should the agent text a booking link, or book the appointment while the patient is on the line?

What Rosie documents that matters here

  • Rosie's pricing page lists Professional at $49 a month for 250 minutes, Scale at $149 for 1,000 minutes and Growth at $299 for 2,000 minutes, with a 7-day free trial and two months free on annual billing. Source official page fetched, 2026-10-28
  • Professional sends appointment links by text; Scale adds booking directly on your calendar, warm transfers where Rosie briefs your team before connecting, and texts during the call. Source official page fetched, 2026-10-28
  • All plans list English and Spanish on every call, simultaneous calls, summaries, transcripts and recordings, and a Zapier integration. Source official page fetched, 2026-10-28

Why it fits dental & medical clinics

Many dental patients happily book online, but new patients and people in pain usually want a time confirmed before they hang up. That is exactly where Rosie splits its plans. Spanish on every plan helps practices in bilingual neighbourhoods, where a Spanish-speaking caller who reaches voicemail often books elsewhere. Warm transfer on Scale lets the agent tell the front desk 'new patient, cracked molar, wants today' before the call connects. (Our analysis.)

What it costs at dental & medical clinics volumes

Our arithmetic: 400 calls at 3 minutes is 1,200 minutes a month, more than Scale's 1,000. Rosie's pricing page did not show an overage rate when we checked, so confirm it or budget for Growth at $299. A smaller office with 250 three-minute calls (750 minutes) fits Scale at $149.

Setup outline for dental & medical clinics

  1. Ask Rosie in writing whether it signs a HIPAA business associate agreement, and on which plan, before routing patient calls.
  2. Decide link versus live booking: if your scheduling software takes online bookings, test Professional; if not, start on Scale.
  3. Teach Rosie your FAQs in English and Spanish: insurance accepted, new-patient offers, parking, hours, emergency policy.
  4. Set warm-transfer rules for pain, swelling, trauma and anyone asking for the dentist by name.
  5. Run the 7-day trial on after-hours calls and have a fluent reviewer read the Spanish transcripts too.
  6. Multiply the trial week's minutes by four and ask how minutes beyond the plan are billed before picking a tier.

Our suggested order, not vendor documentation.

Watch-outs

  • No overage rate was shown on the pricing page; ask before a busy month.
  • Texted booking links only work if patients finish booking; track completion.
  • Settle HIPAA questions before go-live.
  • Keep clinical advice out of the FAQ answers.
