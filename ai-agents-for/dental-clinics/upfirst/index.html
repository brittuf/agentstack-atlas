Upfirst for dental & medical clinics: setup outline and ROI preset

Upfirst bills per call with small steps between plans, and it is one of the few receptionist pages that names HIPAA business associate agreements. That detail matters for dental, because it shows which plan tier you would have to be on.

What Upfirst documents that matters here

  • Upfirst's pricing page lists Starter at $24.95/month for 30 calls, Premium at $59.95 for 90, Pro at $159.95 for 300 and Scale at $299 for 600, with annual billing about 20% lower and extra calls costing $1.50, $1, 75¢ and 70¢. Source official page fetched, 2026-11-11
  • The page lists Business Associate Agreements for HIPAA-regulated businesses under the Enterprise plan (1,000+ calls, custom pricing), and says spam never counts toward call volume. Source official page fetched, 2026-11-11
  • Features on every plan include two-way texting, 35+ languages, warm and waterfall transfers, scheduling into Google, Outlook, Clio, Acuity, Calendly and Square, and a 14-day free trial with no card. Source official page fetched, 2026-11-11

Why it fits dental & medical clinics

A dental office wants answers about hours, new patients and insurance, and a person for anything involving pain. Upfirst's per-call steps suit a small practice that gets a few hundred calls. The compliance question is the catch: by the page's own listing, BAAs sit on the Enterprise tier, which starts at 1,000 calls. A single-dentist office is likely below that, so it must ask directly whether a BAA is available lower down, or use the agent only for non-patient-specific calls. (Our analysis.)

What it costs at dental & medical clinics volumes

Our arithmetic from the posted prices: 400 calls a month on Pro is $159.95 plus 100 extra calls at 75¢, about $235 billed monthly, or roughly $203 on the annual rate of $128. Scale at $299 covers 600 calls.

Setup outline for dental & medical clinics

  1. Ask Upfirst whether a BAA is offered on any plan below Enterprise and what that costs; the page only lists it under Enterprise.
  2. Start the 14-day trial with a script limited to hours, location, new-patient intake and booking, with no patient-specific details taken.
  3. Connect the scheduling tool you already use if it is on the supported list, or use text confirmations to start.
  4. Write escalation rules: pain, bleeding or a broken tooth goes by warm or waterfall transfer to the on-call phone.
  5. Count calls and extra-call charges across the trial, then decide between monthly and annual billing.
  6. Review transcripts weekly for the first month and keep a human callback list for after-hours messages.

Our suggested order, not vendor documentation.

Watch-outs

  • The HIPAA BAA listing is under Enterprise only; do not assume it covers a smaller plan.
  • Extra calls add up in busy months; watch the call count weekly.
  • Waterfall transfers need current phone numbers for each person on the list.
  • Keep the agent from collecting health details until compliance is settled.
