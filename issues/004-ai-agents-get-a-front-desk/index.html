Agents at Work #4: AI agents are getting a front desk

Week of November 5–12, 2026 · about a 6-minute read

This week's theme: in issue #1, agents got passports. In #2, they got budgets. In #3, they got performance reviews. This week, they got a front desk. OpenAI, NiCE and ZEPIC all shipped ways for a business to put an agent in front of customers, inside ChatGPT, on WhatsApp, or in the contact center. First, a company that has been doing it in stages, and has the numbers to show for it.

Deployment of the week: Sling TV's AI Agent (built with Pypestream)

Sling TV's AI Agent works in web and mobile chat. It answers questions, completes signups, and hands off to a human agent "with full context when needed." In one conversation, a fan can ask when a game is on and then pick a package, create an account, pay and change the order. Per a Pypestream press release (Nov 12), comparing Q3 2026 with Q3 2025:

  • Resolution rose from 36% to 71%.
  • CSAT rose from 42% to 72%.
  • Escalations fell 29%.
  • Chat sales conversion rose 9 points, from 15.7% to 24.9%.

The agent covers "an ever-growing list of 50 use cases," powered by 18+ system integrations, including Adobe Commerce.

Caveats: this is a vendor press release from Pypestream, which builds the agent, based on a Nov 10 VUX World webinar. The release doesn't say how resolution is measured, and the demo shown in the session was "a scripted recreation" with fictional customer details.

What makes it work: patience. Sling TV has worked with Pypestream since 2016 and built the agent "one phase at a time": first AI summaries for human agents, then intent-accuracy checks, then single-turn FAQ answers (August 2025), then multi-turn conversations (March 2026). "Nothing goes live without defined success metrics," and every experience is graded on accuracy, relevancy, tone and next best action. More on that in the how-to tip below.

Source: Pypestream via Newswire, Nov 12, 2026

This week in agentic AI

1. OpenAI puts Business Agents inside ChatGPT (Nov 10). OpenAI launched Businesses in ChatGPT in beta. Through Business Hub, a company can claim its business, update details, add product feeds with pricing and availability, and see aggregated mentions, website clicks and "discovery topics." Eligible U.S. ecommerce and online service businesses can also build, test and publish a Business Agent that customers can choose to chat with from the business's ChatGPT profile. OpenAI says those chats are clearly labeled, kept separate from ChatGPT's own answers, and not shared with the business. For now, per OpenAI's help center, the agent explains products and policies and links to your website; it can't complete purchases, make bookings or check orders. OpenAI says it plans to expand to more markets and categories over time. OpenAI

2. NiCE launches the next generation of NiCE Cognigy (Nov 12). NiCE's contact-center agent platform now builds agents from reusable "skills, tools and personas," coordinates sub-agents for complex work, and connects with external agents, "including customers' personal AI agents, through open standards." The release adds AI Assist for setup and testing, step-by-step tracing of every decision, and speech-to-speech voice. NiCE says default AI models are included in the conversation price, with no separate model contract, and that existing deployments keep running unchanged. NiCE

3. ZEPIC launches Neura, a "conversational storefront" (Nov 12). Neura is one agent for the whole shopping journey, from an ad, a Google Business Profile link to WhatsApp, or a brand's site chat. ZEPIC says it answers questions, checks stock, points shoppers to a nearby store, and handles offers, returns and order changes in tools like Shopify, Magento and the brand's CRM. Brands brief it "the way they would brief someone on their first day," and it groups the questions it couldn't answer so the brand knows what to add next. ZEPIC says Neura is available now worldwide on web chat, SMS and WhatsApp, with Instagram and voice "coming soon." ZEPIC via GlobeNewswire

4. A cautionary tale: NEAR AI breach reaches 18 agents (reported Nov 10). Per The Hacker News, an attacker exploited a remote code execution flaw in SGLang, an open-source inference engine, on one of NEAR AI's inference servers. The attacker took chat history, settings and logs from 18 AI agents on an older deployment, plus account details for about 100 users of a deprecated product; two customers' API keys were exposed in logs and have been revoked. The company says it patched its servers, replaced all agent tokens and rotated credentials. The Hacker News

The pattern (our take): the agent is moving from the back office to the front counter. A customer may now meet your business first as an agent, in ChatGPT, in a WhatsApp thread, or on the phone. That changes the job. A back-office agent that gets something wrong costs you time. A front-desk agent that gets something wrong costs you a customer, in public. All three launches stress the same things: answers grounded in the business's own information, a human handoff, and visibility into what the agent did or couldn't answer. And the NEAR AI incident is a reminder that an agent's chat history and logs are customer data. Old deployments you forgot about still count.

Tool spotlight: Retell AI

Retell AI builds voice agents that "answer and make phone calls, take action in your systems, and scale with your call volume." Its site lists call transfer, appointment booking, a knowledge base, phone-tree navigation, post-call analysis and simulation testing, and says it's "in production at 4,000+ businesses." Per its pricing page, pay-as-you-go starts at $0 with $10 in free credits, then $0.07–$0.31 per minute for voice agents and $0.002+ per message for chat agents, with 20 free concurrent calls. Enterprise pricing is custom.

A first build in the spirit of this week: an after-hours receptionist for a local service business. Load your hours, service area, prices and FAQ into the knowledge base → let it book into your calendar → transfer to a human for anything about billing or emergencies → review post-call analysis every Monday for questions it couldn't answer. How-to tip: put your agent on probation before it meets customers

Sling TV didn't flip a switch. Per the Pypestream release, here's what it did, and you can copy it at any size:

  1. Set the bar before launch. Advice from Sling TV's Jana Johnson (Senior Manager of Digital Care and Operations at EchoStar): "set baseline metrics and a success goal before launch, then keep monitoring them." Write down today's resolution rate, escalation rate and CSAT (or a simple thumbs-up count) before the agent goes live.
  2. Start behind the counter. Sling TV began with AI summaries for its human agents and validated intent accuracy before anything went customer-facing. Every new capability is "vetted and tested with human agents and employees before customers ever see it."
  3. Ground answers in what's already public. Grounding answers in Sling TV's public help center "made approval from legal and other teams straightforward." If it isn't on your FAQ page, the agent shouldn't be saying it.
  4. Keep a weekly review. A Sling TV content analyst works with Pypestream weekly, and daily in peak seasons. "AI is not set and forget."
  5. Always offer a human. The release cites a Gartner survey of 3,566 customers (Aug 4, 2026) in which 87% said companies using GenAI must offer an option to reach a human agent.

A small-team version: before launch, run your 20 most common customer questions through the agent and check every answer by hand. OpenAI's own setup guide for Business Agents suggests the same habit: preview with a question customers often ask, and if the answer is wrong, fix the information and test again.
