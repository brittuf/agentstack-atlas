Agents at Work #6: AI agents are getting rules of the road

Week of November 19–26, 2026 · about a 6-minute read

This week's theme: agents got passports in #1, budgets in #2, performance reviews in #3, a front desk in #4 and job descriptions in #5. This week they got rules of the road. A UK regulator spelled out who answers for an agent, 51 authors proposed license plates for agents, and three launches put a stop sign in front of the risky moves. Our deployment of the week shows the other half: an agent that passed a road test before it took a real call.

Deployment of the week: Candid Health's payer-call agents (built on ElevenAgents)

Candid Health is a revenue cycle management platform for some of the largest U.S. healthcare organizations. When a denied claim needs a call to the insurer, a biller can spend "anywhere from 10 minutes to an hour" on that one call. Candid now has voice agents on ElevenLabs' ElevenAgents platform make those calls to investigate denied claims and verify patient eligibility. Per ElevenLabs' customer story (Nov 19):

  • In the initial deployment the agents handled 12,860 calls and saved more than 850 hours of staff time per month.
  • They resolved 59% of the piloted calls. A call only counts as resolved if it finds why the claim was denied and the next step to fix it.
  • The average call lasted 8 minutes and 46 seconds.
  • Candid's engineers integrated ElevenAgents within 48 hours, including telephony, APIs and post-call webhooks.

Caveats: this is a vendor case study from ElevenLabs, which sells the platform. It doesn't say over what period the calls were made or how the 850 hours were measured. COO Doug Proctor says the 59% "exceeded our expectations" this early.

What makes it work: a fixed route and a road test. Candid built the agents "to investigate denials, not to appeal them." Each follows a detailed questioning protocol, adjusted to each insurer's phone tree. Before an agent goes live, it calls a Candid biller who plays the insurance rep, and every call is scored against evaluation criteria. Billers edit the prompts themselves.

This week in agentic AI

1. The UK's FCA: the agent is still your responsibility (Nov 24). The Financial Conduct Authority set out expectations for firms using agentic AI in financial services. If a regulated firm deploys an agent, including a third-party one, "responsibility for complying with regulatory obligations remains with you and senior managers within your firm." Firms must understand "the degree of autonomy those systems have" and the controls needed "in real time and in stress situations," and should decide whether to accept payments instructed by a customer's AI agent. Calling yourself a technology provider, the FCA adds, doesn't mean you don't need authorization. FCA

2. 51 authors propose "license plates" for AI agents (Nov 24). The Center for AI Safety and co-authors published the Accountable Agents Framework. Trusted directories would verify the person or company behind an agent and issue it IDs. "Like a license plate, an ID means nothing on its own, but in cases of harm, it can be traced back to a responsible principal." Agents can show a different ID on each site so they can't be tracked, and an agent accessing a bank account "might need to prove exactly who they are." It's a proposal; the authors invite platforms, identity services and agent providers to collaborate. Accountable Agents Framework · CAIS on X, Nov 24

3. Meta brings its Muse personal agent to the UK (Nov 23). Meta says Muse can book travel, fill out forms and chase refunds, and keeps working after you close the app. You choose which apps it connects to and how much access each gets; Muse "asks for your go-ahead before important actions, like sending an email or making a purchase," and can't see passwords or payment methods. UK partners include Marks & Spencer, Currys, ASOS, easyJet and Jet2. It's free for adults, with optional paid plans. Meta

4. Pine Labs launches Guide and Marketer Agent for India's small merchants (Nov 25). Guide answers questions about sales, settlements and refunds in plain language. Marketer Agent turns a business goal into an ad campaign, starting on Google platforms. "Merchants review and approve campaigns before any spend is committed." A permission layer called Grantex "controls this access and enables human oversight at critical stages." Pine Labs says both are rolling out to millions of merchants. Pine Labs

5. WeWeb adds agents with approval limits (Nov 26). WeWeb's Agents, in early access, let you pick which functions and integrations an agent can use, "decide which actions need someone's confirmation," and inspect its steps. Its example: customers look up orders and request refunds, "with approval required above a limit you set." In Slack, the requester approves or denies right in the thread. WeWeb

The pattern (our take): last week agents got job descriptions; this week the world around them started writing traffic law. The lines are drawn at money and messages. Muse stops before a purchase or an email, Pine Labs before ad spend, WeWeb above a refund limit, and the FCA singles out payments an agent instructs. Below that line, agents drive themselves. For a small business that's a useful map: let the agent look things up, draft and investigate freely, and put a human stop sign in front of anything that spends money or speaks for you. And the FCA's point applies beyond finance: responsibility doesn't transfer with the software.

Tool spotlight: Cloudflare Search for Agents

Rules of the road need road signs. Cloudflare's Search for Agents, in beta since Nov 25, lets agents search your website instead of crawling it. Cloudflare says one dashboard toggle indexes your site, exposes a search endpoint on your own domain at /.well-known/search, and "automatically advertises the search endpoint on every page." It "returns retrieved content, in Markdown, not a generated answer." Requests still pass through your AI Crawl Control and firewall settings.

It's available on all plans, including Free with limits. Per Cloudflare's pricing page, AI Search billing begins December 1, 2026; each account gets 5 million ingestion tokens, 10 GB-month of storage and 1,000 semantic plus 1,000 full-text queries a month included, then semantic queries cost $0.75 per 1,000. The Workers Free plan crawls up to 500 pages a day.

A first build in the spirit of this week: turn it on for a local business site already on Cloudflare, then query the three things customers ask most (hours, prices, service area). If the right passage doesn't come back, fix the page. It pairs with our GEO playbook.

How-to tip: write your agent's rules of the road

Before an agent touches a customer, write five rules. Each comes from this week's sources:

  1. A plate. Give every agent a name and an owner, and keep a list. The FCA's good-practice review describes firms that "registered their agents in a central inventory" showing which are live, who owns them and what they can access.
  2. Speed limits. Set the dollar line above which a human approves: refunds, purchases, ad spend.
  3. A fixed route. Script the job, the way Candid's agents investigate denials but don't appeal them.
  4. A road test. Have a teammate play the customer and score every test call against written criteria before launch.
  5. The owner. Decide who answers when the agent gets it wrong. Per the FCA, buying from a vendor doesn't change who that is.
