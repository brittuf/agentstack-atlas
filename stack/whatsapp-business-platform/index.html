Where it fits in an agent build

Drivers, technicians and many customers live in WhatsApp, not in your software. UNACEM's result came from bringing scheduling and status to drivers where they already were. The pricing structure also suits agents: when a customer starts the conversation, replies inside the 24-hour window are service messages and are not charged, so an agent that answers inbound questions costs much less than one that sends outbound promotions. LUXGEN's LINE agent is the same idea in a market where LINE dominates. (Our analysis.)

When to look elsewhere

In markets where customers expect SMS or a website chat, WhatsApp adds friction. Outbound marketing messages are charged and governed by opt-in rules, so an agent that pushes promotions needs consent tracking before it needs a model. (Our analysis.)

A setup outline

  1. Choose one inbound job your customers or field staff already message about (status, booking, opening hours).
  2. Get a Business Platform number through Meta or a provider, and set up opt-in collection.
  3. Keep replies short and structured; field users often have poor connectivity.
  4. Hand off to a person with the full thread attached when the agent is unsure.
  5. Track message categories monthly so costs stay mostly in the free service window.
