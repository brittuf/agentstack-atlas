Where it fits in an agent build

Freddy suits an email-heavy queue where most questions are policy or how-to answers already written down somewhere. Because a session costs the same whether Freddy solves it or hands off, the math rewards high deflection: by our arithmetic, 300 AI sessions a month cost three packs, $147, which is $0.49 a session against $0.99 to $2.00 per resolution elsewhere in our tracker. The 72-hour email window also means a back-and-forth thread counts once. Seats start at $19, so a two-person office can try it without a big platform bill. (Our analysis.)

When to look elsewhere

Per-session billing charges for failures too, so a weak knowledge base burns money fast. The 500 free sessions are a one-time trial allowance, not a monthly grant, so do not budget them twice. If most of your customers write in on Instagram or WhatsApp rather than email, check which channels your Freshdesk edition covers; standalone Freshdesk accounts get only the email AI agent. (Our analysis.)

Questions to ask before you buy

  • Do unused session packs carry over if we pay annually?
  • Which channels does the AI agent cover on our edition beyond email?
  • How are escalated sessions reported, so we can see what we paid for without a resolution?
  • Is the MCP action add-on needed for order or account lookups, or do native connectors cover them?

A setup outline

  1. Publish or clean up the solution articles customers should see; only publicly visible articles feed the agent.
  2. Create one Email AI Agent in AI Agent Studio and point it at a single support mailbox.
  3. Add policy PDFs and a few Q&A pairs for questions your articles miss.
  4. Test in the studio until answers cite the right article, then deploy to that mailbox only.
  5. Watch how fast the 500 free sessions go; that is your real monthly session rate.
  6. Buy session packs to match, and add MCP actions only for lookups the agent cannot answer from documents.
