Where it fits in an agent build

Synthflow fits a contact center or multi-site operator that has outgrown a self-serve receptionist: many concurrent calls, its own phone system over SIP, and a security review before anything goes live. The pricing page reads like a scoping checklist (concurrency, telephony, integrations, security, launch support), which is roughly the list of decisions Travelers and Maccabi had to make. Write your answer to each item before the first sales call and you already have a requirements document. (Our analysis.)

When to look elsewhere

At a $30,000-a-year floor, a single-location small business is better served by the receptionists in our pricing tracker, most of which start under $100 a month. Teams that want to build and tinker without a contract should look at developer voice platforms such as ElevenLabs' agent tools, or no-code receptionists with free trials. (Our analysis.)

Questions to ask before you buy

  • What does the $30,000 floor include in minutes, concurrency and support hours?
  • Is there a paid pilot before the annual commitment?
  • Who builds and maintains the integrations, and do we keep them if we leave?
  • Will you sign our DPA and complete our security review before the pilot starts?

A setup outline

  1. Measure peak concurrent calls, not just monthly volume; concurrency drives the quote.
  2. Decide whether calls stay on your current carrier through SIP trunking or move to Synthflow's telephony.
  3. List every system the agent must read or write (CRM, calendar, contact-center software) and who owns each integration.
  4. Write escalation and fallback rules before the pilot, including what the agent does when a connected system is down.
  5. Pilot one queue, compare containment and transfer rates with human-handled calls, then negotiate the full contract.
