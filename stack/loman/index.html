Where it fits in an agent build

For pizza, wings, Chinese takeout and other phone-order-heavy kitchens, the expensive moment is a ringing phone during the rush. An agent that takes the full order, charges the card and sends a ticket to the kitchen through the POS removes that interruption, provided the menu mapping is right. Flat monthly pricing, if confirmed, suits restaurants whose call count spikes on Fridays, because the bill does not move with volume. (Our analysis.)

When to look elsewhere

Fine-dining rooms whose calls are mostly reservations may get more from a reservation-first tool. If your POS is not on the list, integration is the first question. The $199 figure comes from Loman's blog while its pricing page asks for a quote, so get the price of the plan with ordering in writing. (Our analysis.)

Questions to ask before you buy

  • What is the monthly price of Premium, which includes ordering and payment?
  • How does the agent handle allergy questions the menu data cannot answer?
  • How quickly do menu changes and sold-out items reach the agent?
  • Which processor handles card details taken over the phone?

A setup outline

  1. Confirm your POS is supported and that orders arrive as normal tickets with modifiers intact.
  2. Send the full menu with sizes, modifiers and combos and review how the agent reads each one back.
  3. Place 20 test orders including substitutions, an allergy question and an item the kitchen cannot make.
  4. Turn it on for overflow calls during one busy service before it takes every call.
  5. Compare phone revenue, remakes and missed calls for four weeks against the prior month.
