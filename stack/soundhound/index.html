Where it fits in an agent build

SoundHound is built for chains: noisy drive-thru audio, menu vocabulary across thousands of locations, and POS integration so orders go straight to the kitchen. Employee Assist is the less obvious part. A barista asks a headset how many pumps of syrup go in a drink and hears the handbook answer, which is an internal-ops agent in a restaurant. The vendor's performance figures are worth testing in a reference call before they go into any business case. (Our analysis.)

When to look elsewhere

An independent restaurant with one phone line is not SoundHound's buyer; pricing is not public and the logos are national chains. Smaller operators should look at phone-ordering tools that sign up online. Drive-thru automation changes the guest experience, so test with real customers before rolling it across locations. (Our analysis.)

Questions to ask before you buy

  • Which POS systems are integrated natively, and which need custom work?
  • How were the 11% and 32% figures measured, and can we speak to a reference customer?
  • Does pricing scale with locations, orders or minutes?
  • Which languages are supported on the phone channel?

A setup outline

  1. Pick one channel (phone or drive-thru) and two pilot locations with different traffic.
  2. Export the full menu with modifiers and check it maps cleanly into the POS integration.
  3. Agree on what counts as an order error and measure it with mystery orders before and after.
  4. Train staff on when to take over and how hand-offs work.
  5. Decide on rollout only after a full month that includes a peak week.
