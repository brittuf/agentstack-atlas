Where it fits in an agent build

Vapi fits a builder who wants low lock-in: you pick each provider, pay them at Vapi's listed pass-through cost, and Vapi takes $0.05 a minute for hosting. By our arithmetic from the page ranges, an all-in rate lands between about $0.082 and $0.129 a minute, which matches Vapi's own 1,000-minute estimate. The built-in API request tool means the agent can look up or write to a job system or CRM that has an API, which is how an agency would plug it into a small shop's existing software. (Our analysis.)

When to look elsewhere

The free tier's 4 concurrent calls and 14-day data retention are fine for testing but thin for a busy line, and an SLA only starts with Core. Teams that need HIPAA should price the HIPAA add-on first: on 2026-11-25 the pricing page listed it at $2,000 a month, which outweighs the hosting fee for a small clinic. Owners who want a finished product rather than a toolkit should start with the receptionists in our pricing tracker. (Our analysis.)

Questions to ask before you buy

  • Does the $2,000-a-month HIPAA add-on cover every number and organization we run?
  • Which providers keep our per-minute cost near the low end of the range without hurting latency?
  • Do we need Core's 2-business-day SLA, or is community support enough to start?
  • Who maintains the assistant and its tools after launch?

A setup outline

  1. Write down your peak simultaneous calls; above 4 you need Core or concurrency add-ons.
  2. Create an assistant from the Customer Support or Appointment Scheduler template in the dashboard.
  3. Choose transcriber, model and voice and price them with the calculator on Vapi's pricing page.
  4. Add tools: transfer call to the on-call number, SMS through your Twilio account, and an API request into your booking system.
  5. Test with scripted calls, including a caller who interrupts and one who needs a human.
  6. Go live on one number for after-hours calls and check call logs for the Call Cost breakdown weekly.
